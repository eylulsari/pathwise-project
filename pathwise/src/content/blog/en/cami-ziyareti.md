Most mosques in Istanbul are open to visitors and free to enter. But they are
also places of worship, and that is where the rules of a visit come from. Here
we open out the four points from Pathwise's practical-information page.

## It may be closed at prayer times

There are five daily prayers, and the mosque may close to visitors at each of
them. The closure is not long — usually around half an hour — but finding out
when you reach the door is a nuisance.

The times shift with the season, because they follow the sun. Friday midday
prayer is separate: the crowd is bigger and the closure lasts longer.

## Shoulders and knees covered

This applies to both women and men. Women also wear a headscarf.

You do not have to prepare for this in advance: **most mosques hand out a
covering at the entrance**, free and returned afterwards. If you have arrived
in shorts or a sleeveless top, a skirt or shawl is usually offered too.

## Shoes come off

There are racks at the entrance, or a bag is handed out. Where the carpet
begins marks the line.

A practical note: shoes that come off easily make a real difference if you are
visiting two or three mosques in a day.

## Do not walk in front of someone praying, and do not use flash

This is the point most often missed. Even while a mosque is open to visitors
there may be people praying inside — walking in front of them interrupts the
prayer. Going around behind them is enough.

Photography is generally allowed, but **no flash**.

## In practice: the Blue Mosque (Sultanahmet Camii)

The most visited mosque in the city, and where the rules are at their most
visible: coverings handed out at the entrance, shoe bags, and signage. Because
it is crowded, the prayer-time closures are also felt most here.

## In practice: Süleymaniye Mosque

Quieter, and for that reason usually easier to visit. Because it stands on one
of the highest hills in the city, its courtyard is also a viewpoint — a lot of
people come at sunset.
